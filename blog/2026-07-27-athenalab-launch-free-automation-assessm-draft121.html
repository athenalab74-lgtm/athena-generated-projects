How Many Times Did Someone Ask 'Where Is My Delivery?' This Week?

If you run a workshop, a logistics company, or any business that deals with customers directly, you already know the answer. It's probably more than you'd like to admit.

Here's the pattern I see in almost every SME I talk to: the same five or six questions come in over and over again. Where's my delivery? Is my car ready? What are your hours? Can I get a quote? Someone on your team answers these one by one, all day, every day, on WhatsApp, on the phone, in person.

None of it is hard work. All of it is necessary. And all of it eats time that could go toward actual business - fixing cars, moving freight, closing sales.

Let's put a number on it. If a staff member spends even one hour a day on repetitive replies, that's five hours a week. Over a month, that's roughly 20 hours - half a working week - spent typing the same answers instead of doing paid work or helping a customer with something that actually needs a human.

The cost isn't just time. It's the customer waiting longer than they should for a simple answer. It's your staff getting pulled away from higher-value tasks to handle something a computer could do in seconds. It's slower response times during your busiest hours, which is exactly when customers are least patient and most likely to go elsewhere.

Multiply that across a team, across a year, and you're looking at real money - not from one big mistake, but from hundreds of small, repeated ones.
